# Graphics

*[Versión en español](../es/graphics.md) · [All pages](README.md)*

The mod looks right in two modes: plain Minecraft (with or without Sodium) and with a
shader pack through Iris. It detects which one is in use **live**
(`client.ShaderMode`, through Iris's public API by reflection, since Iris is optional), so
turning shaders on or off while playing switches at once.

## The flashlight

The beam's shape is pure math, shared by both modes: `systems.FlashlightBeam`.

- A cone from the player's eyes in the look direction, `beam_width_degrees` wide (40),
  reaching `beam_range` (20 blocks).
- Light dims from `beam_brightness` (15) at the lens to 0 at the far end, and fades by a
  few levels per block outside the cone, so the edge is soft.
- It stops just past the first block it hits, so it lights the wall in front and not the
  room behind it.
- A faint glow (`glow_brightness`, 5) around the player.

`client.effects.FlashlightEffect` recomputes the beam every client tick while the server
says the flashlight is on, including the low-battery flicker. `client.compat.LambDynamicLightsCompat`
turns it into **block light** through LambDynamicLights (a required dependency; without it
the flashlight would light nothing and the player is told once).

### Without shaders

Block light shows its steps, and every change rebuilds chunk sections. So:

- The light is recomputed at most 5 times a second, and only for bigger changes (fewer
  chunk rebuilds, no stutter).
- `client.hud.FlashlightOverlay` darkens the screen around a round spot in the middle,
  slightly warm at its heart (`flashlight_spot.png`, `flashlight_glow.png`, from
  `tools/gui/flashlight_spot.py`). The spot lags behind fast camera turns, like a
  hand-held light, and settles back; it follows the flicker and dimming.

### With shaders

The pack smooths block light, so the beam is shaped as a flashlight instead
(`FlashlightBeam.create(forShaders = true)`): a narrower cone, a hot spot brighter in the
middle, and less glow around the player. The on-screen spot is off.

## Recommended shader settings

Tuned for **Complementary Reimagined** (Unbound works too). `client.ShaderSettings` writes
these into every Complementary pack in `shaderpacks/` when the client starts, once per pack
and per version of these settings (remembered in
`config/every_tunnel/shader_settings_applied.txt`):

- **Colored lighting off.** It lights the world from the pack's own list of glowing blocks,
  which has none of the mod's, so the fluorescent lights and the flashlight would light
  nothing.
- **World Space Reflections off.** The pack refuses to run them without colored lighting
  and shows an error screen.

What the player changes afterwards is kept, until the recommended settings themselves
change. For the material maps to show, set "RP Support" to "labPBR" in the pack.

Other shader packs may need their own settings; only Complementary is handled.

## Brightness

`LightmapTextureManagerMixin`: in the levels the Brightness option counts as **Moody**,
whatever it is set to, so the dark is dark on every screen. The option itself is never
changed; outside the levels it works as usual.

## Lights

`platform.FluorescentLightBlock`: a shallow two-tube fixture on any face.

- Mode `on`: light level **13** (not 15: at 15 the garage was lit evenly, with no pools of
  light, and the flashlight, capped at 15 like all block light, hardly showed; not 10: with
  Moody brightness the lights seemed off). And a hum. A lit fixture is drawn at full
  brightness (the vanilla `emissiveLighting` of magma), so it always looks on.
- Mode `flicker`: mostly lit, with bursts of rapid off/on switching every few seconds; the
  light really goes out, so the room flickers.
- Mode `off`: dark and silent.
- In Creative, right-click cycles the modes (sneak to place against it).

A few props glow faintly too: the EXIT sign (7), the CCTV monitor (4), floor indicators,
button panels and card readers (1 to 3). The keycard puts out every light in
Level 2 (`KeycardHooks`).

## Textures and models

- **16×16 pixel art** everywhere, generated by Python with seeds (see [Python tools](tools.md)).
- **LabPBR 1.3 maps** next to each block texture: `_n` (normal, AO, height) and `_s`
  (smoothness, F0/metal, porosity, emission), read by shader packs. See `tools/map/garage/pbr.py`.
- **The garage itself** uses re-skinned vanilla blocks: the world resource pack
  (`resources.zip`) overrides the blockstates of a list of stand-in blocks, pointing them at
  its own models in the `etg` namespace. It never replaces a vanilla texture file, and only
  applies in that world.
- **The mod's own blocks** (props, doors, lights, signs) have their assets in the mod jar.
- **`SignTileBlock`** only knows numbered slots; the world resource pack maps each
  (facing, slot) to that level's painted text.
- **The Pale man** is a GeckoLib model with a glow mask for his eyes.

## The menu scene

Behind the main menu, a parking hall seen down its aisle from a fixed camera
(`client.menu.MenuScene`), with no world loaded.

- **Where it comes from:** `tools/map/map_generator.py` builds one hall with the level's own
  code (`MENU_SCENE`, `MENU_CAMERA`; `garage/menu_scene.py`): a closed emergency door under a
  lit EXIT sign at the far end. It writes `assets/every_tunnel/menu/scene.json` (camera,
  palette, blocks) into the mod.
- **Re-skinned blocks:** the world resource pack only applies inside the world, so for each
  stand-in block the scene names the model the pack would pick, copied under
  `every_tunnel:block/menu/` (nothing outside the menu changes). A model loading plugin loads
  them; turned variants go through `RotatedModel`.
- **Drawing:** Minecraft's block renderer draws the blocks from a stand-in world
  (`MenuSceneView`) into one vertex buffer per render layer, drawn with a perspective camera
  into the GUI. `vanish_x` shifts the picture so the far end sits right of the menu's black.
  Fog fades the far end to black. Shaders do not apply in menus: it looks like vanilla.
- **Light:** its own block light (`core.menu.BlockLight`) and its own lightmap: the levels'
  Moody curve with vanilla's warm tint. Lit lamps are drawn at full brightness, as in game.
- **Lamps:** each working lamp flickers every 20 to 40 s (`core.menu.LampFlicker`), the same
  rhythm as a flickering light in the levels; dead ones stay dark. Each switch recomputes the
  light and rebuilds the buffers. No sound.
- **The black:** `MainMenuScreen.drawShade` keeps black solid behind the logo and options (at
  least 30 % of the width), then fades it out smoothly until 65 %.

## Art direction

- A cold, sickly palette: cold, worn concrete, stains of rust, oil, damp and mould.
- A Minecraft-style HUD (vanilla icon sizes, outlined, lit from the top left).
- The only screen effect is the exhaustion vignette: no fog, grain or blur.
- The player is the night guard: black uniform with yellow details.
- Film-like credits: slow roll on black.
- The main menu: black, a worn white serif logo, minimal.
